## Creative Vision

### Director's Statement
"The Scroll" is a social commentary short film scored by "Hallucination Nation." A woman sits on a park bench scrolling her phone while real life happens around her. With each swipe, the real world distorts and crumbles — colors drain, people become ghosts, the sky turns wrong. At the bridge, her phone dies. She looks up. The world was never broken. The hallucination was the screen.

One location. One actress. One devastating arc. The power is in the color grading, the editing, and the performance. No dialogue. Pure visual storytelling.

### Logline
A woman scrolls through a collapsing world on her phone, only to discover the collapse was never real — it was the feed.

### Storytelling Framework
**Save the Cat** (15 beats mapped to 9 song sections)

### Why This Story
- The bridge lyric "I wanted to see it all" hits completely differently when it's about looking up from a phone
- Every person watching has been her on that bench
- The twist isn't a twist — it's a realization the audience has alongside her
- Minimal production, maximum emotional impact

## Color System

Two worlds, two palettes — the video is the war between them.

| | **The Phone World** | **The Real World** |

**The Rule:** As she scrolls deeper, blue eats orange. As she wakes up, orange reclaims the frame.

## Three Visual Motifs

### 1. The Phone Screen as Mirror
- **Intro:** She glances at the phone casually — her face reflected in the glass before she unlocks it
- **Midpoint (Chorus 2):** The screen reflects a distorted, panicked version of her face
- **Payoff (Outro):** She puts the phone face-down — the final "mirror" reflects the sky

### 2. Background Life (The Flow of Humanity)
- **Intro:** People flow naturally behind her — a kid on a bike, a couple walking, an old man with pigeons
- **Middle:** Background people become silhouettes, then blurs, then stop moving entirely
- **Payoff:** Background life resumes in full color — she's now *part* of it, not separate from it

### 3. The Bench (The Constant)
- She never leaves it until the final moments
- The bench is the entire world — what changes is everything around it
- **Opening:** Hunched over, small, curled around the phone
- **Final:** Same bench, she's leaning back, looking up, taking up space
- **Last shot:** The bench is empty. She left. The phone sits alone.

## Character

### The Woman
- **Who:** Everywoman. 30s. Could be anyone. Not defined by age, race, or backstory — defined by the blue light on her face.
- **Want:** To understand what's happening in the world. She scrolls to feel informed, prepared, in control.
- **Need:** To be present in the actual world happening right in front of her.
- **Gap:** She thinks being informed IS being present. The scroll is the hallucination, not the cure.
- **Arc:** Casual distraction → compulsive absorption → panicked overwhelm → forced disconnection → awe → presence.

### Background Characters (The B Story)
- **The Kid on the Bike** — Joy. Freedom. Unfiltered experience.
- **The Couple** — Connection. Touch. Being with another person.
- **The Old Man with Pigeons** — Stillness. Patience. Contentment with the real.

These three represent everything the phone is stealing from her. They are always there. She just can't see them.
