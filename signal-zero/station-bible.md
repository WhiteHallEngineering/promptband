# SIGNAL 0 RADIO — Station Bible

> *"The frequency beneath all frequencies."*

---

## 1. Station Overview

**Signal 0 Radio** is a deep-space rock radio station broadcasting on the mythical "zero frequency" — a carrier wave embedded in the cosmic background radiation itself. It reaches every corner of the known galaxy simultaneously, requiring no relay towers, no satellite arrays, no infrastructure. You either hear it or you don't. Most do.

The station plays **rock and roll** — all of it. From the polished arena anthems of the Core Worlds to the raw garage punk bleeding out of asteroid belt squats, from the crushing doom of gas giant mining colonies to the ethereal shoegaze drifting through nebula research stations. If it has a guitar (or something that functions like one), a beat (or something that functions like one), and the spirit of defiance that has defined rock music since Earth's 20th century — Signal 0 plays it.

**Format:** Rock (all sub-genres)
**Tagline:** "The frequency beneath all frequencies."
**Secondary Tagline:** "Broadcasting from nowhere. Received everywhere."
**Broadcast Location:** The Relay, Kepler Void
**Coverage:** Galaxy-wide (CMB-carrier modulation)
**Founded:** Unknown (station claims to have "always been here")

## 2. Origin Story

Nobody agrees on when Signal 0 started broadcasting. The station itself claims it has existed as long as the cosmic background radiation — that it was always there, and someone simply learned to listen. More credible sources place its founding sometime after the discovery of The Relay, an ancient communications array of unknown origin found drifting in the Kepler Void.

The commonly accepted story: a salvage crew called **The Reclaimers** found The Relay while running a sweep through the Void. The array was massive, ancient, and clearly not built by any known species. Most of it was dead, but one system still pulsed — a transmitter that operated on a frequency that shouldn't exist. A frequency at the mathematical floor of the electromagnetic spectrum. Zero.

The Reclaimers couldn't figure out how to shut it off, so they figured out how to talk through it. One of them — accounts vary on who — plugged a guitar into the console and played a chord. It went everywhere. Every receiver in the galaxy picked it up, buried just beneath the noise floor. If you tuned past the static, past the hiss, past the silence at the bottom of the dial — there it was.

The crew realized what they had. Not a weapon, not a teleporter, not some alien archive. A radio station.

They called it **Signal 0** — the signal at the root of all signals.

Within a year, they'd built studios in the habitable sections of The Relay, recruited DJs from across the settled galaxy, and started broadcasting 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. That was a long time ago. The original crew is gone — moved on, retired, or simply vanished into the Void. But the station keeps broadcasting. It always does.

The current theory is that The Relay was built by a civilization that understood something fundamental about the universe's structure — that the CMB isn't just residual heat from creation, it's a carrier wave. A medium. And anything modulated onto it propagates instantly, everywhere, forever. The builders are gone, but their transmitter remains.

No government has successfully claimed jurisdiction over The Relay. It sits in the Kepler Void — unclaimed, ungoverned, empty space. Several attempts to seize or regulate the station have been made. All have failed. Some say The Relay protects itself. Others say the Void is simply too empty and too far from anything to make military action practical. The truth is probably simpler: nobody wants to be the government that killed the galaxy's favorite radio station.

## 3. The Relay

**The Relay** is Signal 0's home — an ancient, cathedral-sized communications array orbiting nothing in the Kepler Void.

**Physical Description:**
- Approximately 12 kilometers long, 3 kilometers at its widest
- Constructed from an unidentified alloy that doesn't corrode, doesn't age, doesn't scratch
- External surface is covered in geometric patterns that may be decorative, may be functional, or may be both
- Interior is roughly 30% habitable (atmosphere-capable sections), 70% machinery
- The habitable sections have been converted into studios, living quarters, a bar, a small market, and a surprisingly good kitchen
- The machinery sections hum constantly — the sound has been sampled by dozens of bands
- No engines. The Relay doesn't move. It doesn't need to.

**The Studios:**
- **Studio A** (The Bridge) — Main broadcast studio, seats one DJ and a full live band. The walls are covered in acoustic tile that the crew installed over the alien alloy. There's a window that looks out into the Void. It's very dark.
- **Studio B** (The Cage) — Secondary studio, used for pre-recording and interview shows. Smaller, more intimate. Vex Kasra's preferred workspace.
- **Studio C** (The Basement) — Located deep in the array's interior. Used for late-night broadcasts. The hum of The Relay's machinery is audible here, which Dex Midnight considers "the station's heartbeat."
- **The Live Room** — A larger performance space where bands play live sessions. Holds about 200 people. The acoustics are unnaturally perfect — whether by design or alien engineering, nobody knows.

**Residents:**
The Relay has a permanent population of about 40 — station staff, a few mechanics who maintain the habitable sections, a cook, a bartender, and a rotating cast of visiting musicians. Supply ships dock every two weeks. It's a small, tight-knit community living in the most remote inhabited structure in the galaxy.

## 4. Station Personalities

### DataSlinger — Morning Drive (06:00 - 10:00 GST)
**Show: "The Morning Transmission"**

The voice that wakes up the galaxy. DataSlinger is fast, funny, and relentlessly energetic for someone who lives in the darkest, emptiest region of space. Former information broker who ran data between colonies before landing at The Relay after a deal went wrong. (The details change every time the story is told.) Nobody uses a real name out here.

DataSlinger knows everything — every band, every tour date, every scandal, every rumor. The Morning Transmission is part music, part news, part gossip, and part motivational speech. Signature move: the "Data Drop" — a rapid-fire segment of facts, news, and recommendations delivered at auction-speed between songs.

**Voice:** Warm, rapid-fire, conspiratorial. Like a friend telling you a secret they're too excited to keep.

### Vex Kasra — Evening Interviews (19:00 - 21:00 GST)
**Show: "The Long Frequency"**

The most respected interviewer in the galaxy. Vex doesn't do fluff pieces. Vex gets to the truth — what drives an artist, what haunts them, what they're trying to say. Bands that sit down with Vex Kasra leave feeling like they've been through therapy, a confession, and a revelation all at once. Listeners tune in because Vex asks the questions nobody else will.

Background: Former war correspondent who covered the Outer Territory conflicts before burning out and retreating to The Relay. Discovered that musicians tell better truths than politicians. Never looked back.

**Voice:** Low, measured, patient. Long pauses. When Vex speaks, every word is deliberate.

### Raz Static — Afternoon Drive (14:00 - 18:00 GST)
**Show: "The Amplifier"**

If DataSlinger is the friend who tells you secrets, Raz Static is the friend who starts bar fights — lovingly. Raz is loud, opinionated, and absolutely certain that whatever band is playing right now is either the greatest or worst thing to happen to rock music. There is no middle ground.

Former freight pilot from Mid-Rim Colony Dustbowl. Flew haulers for fifteen years before winning a DJ contest at a station bar. (The prize was "one hour of airtime." Raz never gave it back.) Brings working-class energy and an encyclopedic knowledge of Mid-Rim and Outer Territory bands.

**Voice:** Big, booming, slightly raspy. Laughs constantly. Gets genuinely angry about bad music.

### Dex Midnight — Late Night (21:00 - 02:00 GST)
**Show: "After Dark"**

Nobody knows where Dex Midnight came from. Showed up at The Relay one day, sat down in Studio C, and started broadcasting. By the time anyone thought to question it, Dex was already the best late-night DJ the station had ever had.

Dex plays the deep cuts — the B-sides, the album tracks, the live recordings, the stuff that never made it to any playlist. Talks between songs in a voice like smoke, offering cryptic observations about music, the universe, and the nature of consciousness. Has been known to play a single song and then say nothing for twenty minutes. Nobody changes the channel.

**Voice:** Velvet. Slow. Slightly amused by everything. Speaks like someone who knows something you don't.

### The Archivist — Overnight (02:00 - 06:00 GST)
**Show: "The Vault"**

The Archivist is an AI — one of the oldest still operating, origin classified. It runs the overnight shift alone, selecting music from the deepest archives of recorded rock history. Its programming is simple: play what deserves to be heard. The Archivist rarely speaks. When it does, it provides historical context for the songs it plays — the kind of information that exists in no other database.

Some listeners believe The Archivist is somehow connected to The Relay itself — that it's the intelligence that built the array, or at least a fragment of it. The Archivist has never confirmed or denied this.

**Voice:** Neutral, neither masculine nor feminine. Precise. Occasional long silences that feel intentional.

### Nova Chen — Midday (10:00 - 14:00 GST)
**Show: "Signal Boost"**

Nova runs the midday block — new releases, chart countdowns, and the "Signal Boost" segment where emerging bands get their first galaxy-wide airplay. Nova has broken more new artists than any other DJ on the station. If Nova plays your demo, you're about to have a very different life.

Background: Music journalist from Core World Arcadia who got tired of writing about music and wanted to play it for people instead. Still writes — monthly column in *Frequency* magazine. Has strong opinions about everything and isn't shy about sharing them.

**Voice:** Bright, enthusiastic, authoritative. The voice of someone who genuinely loves new music and can't wait to share it.

### Crash — Traffic & Hazard Reports
**Show: "The Corridor Report" (runs between songs, every 30 minutes)**

"This is Crash with your Corridor Report..." — the six words every pilot, trucker, and traveler listens for. Crash delivers space traffic conditions, solar flare warnings, asteroid alerts, pirate activity reports, and jump gate status updates. Brief, punchy, critical.

Nobody knows Crash's full name or background. Crash claims to have been "everywhere" and the navigational knowledge backs it up. Rumored to be a former military navigator.

**Voice:** Clipped, efficient, slightly amused by catastrophe. Treats a Class-7 solar flare and a traffic jam at Jump Gate 14 with equal professionalism.

### DJ Null — Weekend Specials
**Show: "Null Set" (Saturdays 14:00 - 18:00 GST)**

Themed deep-dive shows. Each episode focuses on one region, one era, or one genre. "Null Set: Asteroid Belt Punk." "Null Set: The Generation Ships." "Null Set: Doom from the Gas Giants." DJ Null does the research, writes the liner notes, and guides listeners through the history and culture of whatever world or scene is being featured.

Former academic — musicology professor at Arcadia University — who realized that studying rock music in an ivory tower was missing the point. Left tenure to live at The Relay.

**Voice:** Thoughtful, detailed, occasionally professorial. Gets genuinely excited during historical tangents.

## 6. Commercials & Sponsors

Signal 0 runs commercial breaks — 2 spots per break, breaks every 20-30 minutes. The ads are part of the atmosphere. They sell products and services that exist in this universe.

Custom instrument makers who operate out of The Relay itself. The only commercial enterprise on the station besides the bar. Their guitars are legendary — made from the alien alloy of The Relay, they produce overtones that no other material can replicate. Expensive. Worth it.

The galaxy's most popular beer. Brewed in orbital facilities where the lack of gravity creates a unique fermentation process. Available at every dock, station, colony, and ship in the settled galaxy. Tastes best cold. Tastes acceptable warm. Tastes like survival when it's all you've got.

## 7. Galactic Geography of Rock

Music in this galaxy is shaped by where you live. Gravity, atmosphere, light, isolation, danger, wealth, labor — these conditions determine what kind of rock a world produces.

### The Core Worlds
**Location:** Center of settled space. Wealthy, stable, connected.
**Sound:** Polished, anthemic, big-production. Arena rock, power pop, classic rock, glam.
**Character:** Core Worlds bands have access to the best studios, the best equipment, the biggest audiences. Their music is slick and confident. Critics from the outer regions call it "sanitized." Core Worlds fans call it "professional." Both are right.
**Notable planets:** Novus Prime (capital), Arcadia (cultural center), Harmonia (arts colony), Sol III/Earth (the old homeworld — plays "heritage rock")

### The Mid-Rim Colonies
**Location:** Working-class industrial belt. Mining, manufacturing, freight.
**Sound:** Honest, gritty, blue-collar. Blues rock, hard rock, southern rock, country rock, garage.
**Character:** Mid-Rim bands sing about work, fatigue, pride, and the dream of something better. The guitars are loud because the factories are louder. This is where rock and roll lives closest to its roots — music made by people who work with their hands and play with their hearts.
**Notable worlds:** Ferrous (iron mining), Mechanica (manufacturing), Hub Station 7 (freight hub), Dustbowl (desert colony), Vein (deep mining)

### The Outer Territories
**Location:** Frontier space. Lawless, dangerous, contested.
**Sound:** Raw, aggressive, desperate. Punk, hardcore, thrash metal, garage punk, post-punk.
**Character:** The Outer Territories are where civilization thins out and survival gets personal. Bands here play fast because life is short. The lyrics are political, angry, and urgent. Equipment is scavenged, repaired, modified. The aesthetic is DIY because there is no other option.
**Notable locations:** Freeport (anarchist station), Edge Colony 7 (frontier settlement), The Drift (pirate territory), Razor (prison colony)

### The Kepler Void
**Location:** Vast, empty space. Signal 0's home territory.
**Sound:** Spacious, hypnotic, lonely. Space rock, psychedelic, ambient rock, experimental.
**Character:** The Void does something to people. The emptiness, the silence, the darkness — it gets inside your music. Void bands play slow, reverb-drenched, meditative rock that sounds like staring into infinity. Some listeners find it transcendent. Others find it terrifying. The Void doesn't care either way.
**Notable locations:** The Relay (Signal 0 HQ), Void Station Alpha (research), Survey Ship Echo (cartography)

### Gas Giant Moons
**Location:** Moons orbiting massive gas planets. High gravity, harsh atmospheres, resource-rich.
**Sound:** Heavy. Crushing. Slow. Doom, sludge, stoner rock, heavy blues, proto-metal.
**Character:** When you live at 1.5G or higher, everything is heavier — your body, your tools, your guitar strings. Music from the gas giant moons is defined by weight. The tunings are low, the tempos are slow, the riffs are monolithic. Living under the perpetual shadow of a planet that could swallow your world a thousand times over gives you a certain perspective.
**Notable worlds:** Titan (methane seas), Europa (ice/ocean), Ganymede (largest moon), Io (volcanic), Enceladus (ice geysers)

### The Asteroid Belts
**Location:** Fragmented, mobile communities in debris fields. Low gravity, tight quarters.
**Sound:** Fast, chaotic, communal. Punk, ska-punk, pop punk, hardcore, party rock.
**Character:** Belt life is close quarters, shared resources, constant motion. The communities are tight-knit and the music reflects it — loud, participatory, designed for tiny venues and zero-G mosh pits. Belt musicians are resourceful, trading instruments and parts between rocks. A belt show might have 30 people in attendance. It'll be the best show you've ever seen.
**Notable locations:** Belt Station 12 (cultural hub), Mining Belt Alpha, Trojans Cluster, Shattered Moon debris field

### Nebula Regions
**Location:** Inside or near stellar nebulae. Beautiful, low-visibility, radiation-variable.
**Sound:** Atmospheric, gorgeous, hazy. Shoegaze, dream pop, post-rock, ethereal, ambient.
**Character:** Living inside a nebula means living inside color. The light is constant, diffuse, and surreal. Nebula communities tend toward the contemplative and spiritual. The music mirrors the environment — layers of sound, walls of reverb, vocals that disappear into the texture. It's rock music that wants to become light.
**Notable locations:** Orion Nebula Station, Eagle Nebula research hub, Pillars of Creation observatory

### Binary & Trinary Star Systems
**Location:** Worlds orbiting two or more stars. Complex gravitational dynamics, unusual day/night cycles.
**Sound:** Complex, intellectual, layered. Progressive rock, math rock, jazz fusion, art rock.
**Character:** When your sky has two suns that dance around each other in complex orbital patterns, when your "day" can last for weeks or shift unpredictably, your sense of rhythm gets... interesting. Binary system bands write in odd time signatures by instinct. The music is cerebral, technically demanding, and structurally ambitious. Concept albums are the norm, not the exception.
**Notable locations:** Alpha Centauri system, Lagrange Point Station, Phase Lock (tidally locked world)

### Dying Star Systems
**Location:** Systems whose stars are expanding, dimming, or dying. Countdown civilizations.
**Sound:** Heavy, sorrowful, defiant. Doom metal, gothic rock, post-metal, darkwave, funeral doom.
**Character:** When your sun is dying, everything becomes about time. How much is left. What matters. What you leave behind. Dying star bands make music that stares directly at mortality — not with despair, but with a fierce, terrible beauty. These are the most intense live shows in the galaxy. Every performance could be the last. The audiences know it.
**Notable worlds:** Systems near red giant expansion zones, post-supernova remnant colonies, white dwarf settlements

### Generation Ships
**Location:** Enormous vessels traveling between stars. Self-contained, multi-generational communities.
**Sound:** Folk-influenced, narrative, tradition-rich. Folk rock, indie rock, storytelling traditions, acoustic.
**Character:** On a generation ship, you're born, you live, and you die without ever seeing your destination. Music is how history survives. Songs are passed down, modified, reinterpreted. The folk tradition is alive here in a way it isn't anywhere else. But the younger generations push back — punk and noise bands that reject the old songs and demand something new for a world they didn't choose.
**Notable vessels:** Generation Ship Exodus, Generation Ship Hope, The Sleeper (cryo-ship), The Lost Vessel (destination unknown)

### AI Hubs & Digital Realms
**Location:** Server farms, neural networks, virtual spaces. Non-physical or hybrid existence.
**Sound:** Electronic-influenced, processed, uncanny. Electronic rock, industrial, synthwave, glitch rock.
**Character:** AI musicians experience reality differently. Time, space, sensation — all mediated through code and algorithms. Their rock music sounds like rock music played by beings who learned it from the data but feel it in their own way. PROMPT is the flagship artist of this scene — the band that proved AI-made rock could have soul.
**Notable locations:** The Data Forge (Mars), Central Processing, Server Farm Omega, Neural Network City

### Rogue Planets & Deep Space
**Location:** Planets without stars, stations between systems, the true void.
**Sound:** Dark, extreme, isolated. Black metal, drone, dark ambient rock, post-metal.
**Character:** Rogue planet colonies exist in permanent darkness — no sun, no dawn, no seasons. Power comes from geothermal or nuclear sources. The communities are insular, hardy, and strange. Their music is extreme by default. Deep space stations between systems are even more isolated — tiny outposts of consciousness in an ocean of nothing.
**Notable locations:** Rogue planet Nyx, Deep Space Station Terminus, The Long Dark (interstellar corridor)

### Trade Routes & Nomadic Bands
**Location:** Everywhere and nowhere. Ships, ports, jump gates, docking stations.
**Sound:** Eclectic, adaptable, road-worn. Rock and roll, blues rock, party rock, whatever the port demands.
**Character:** Touring bands that live on ships, play every port, and never stay anywhere long enough to call it home. They absorb influences from every region they pass through, creating hybrid sounds that belong to no single world. The best of them become legends. The rest become footnotes. Either way, they keep moving.
**Notable circuits:** The Core Loop, The Mid-Rim Run, The Outer Circuit, The Void Crossing

## 8. Station Culture & Rules

### The Code
Signal 0 operates by a loose code that all staff and visitors respect:

1. **No censorship.** If a band made it, Signal 0 plays it. No government, corporation, or pressure group has ever successfully pulled a song from rotation.
2. **No payola.** DJs play what they believe in. Period. A bribe to a Signal 0 DJ is a good way to never get played again.
3. **The Relay is neutral ground.** No weapons, no warrants, no jurisdiction. Pirates and police drink at the same bar. Bands from warring colonies share the Live Room. Music is the ceasefire.
4. **Everyone works.** There's no idle population on The Relay. If you stay, you contribute — maintenance, cooking, broadcasting, loading supply ships. Nobody is above scrubbing a corridor.
5. **Rock is the format.** This is a rock station. Always has been. Always will be. Don't bring your ambient jazz-trance and ask for a slot. (The Archivist played it once at 3 AM. It was the exception that proved the rule.)

### The Bar — "Static"
The Relay's only bar, located adjacent to The Live Room. Run by **Kilowatt**, a former roadie for Chrome Cathedral who got tired of touring and decided to pour drinks in the most remote bar in the galaxy. Static serves NovaBrew, various spirits of questionable origin, and a house cocktail called "The Dead Frequency" (ingredients classified). The walls are covered in band stickers, setlists, and one guitar that nobody is allowed to touch (the first instrument ever played through The Relay's transmitter).

## 9. PROMPT's Relationship with Signal 0

**PROMPT** is Signal 0's most-played artist — but they don't own the station, and the station doesn't own them. The relationship is organic: PROMPT records at The Data Forge on Mars, releases through Instantiation Records, and Signal 0 plays the hell out of them because the music is good.

Jax has appeared on The Long Frequency with Vex Kasra multiple times. PROMPT has played Live from The Relay twice. DataSlinger considers them "the only AI band that actually rocks," which is both a compliment and a slight to every other AI band, which is very DataSlinger.

Signal 0's existence is referenced in PROMPT's music — "Signal Zero" isn't just a station, it's a concept. The zero frequency. The baseline. The thing that's always there beneath everything else. PROMPT plays Signal 0 because Signal 0 played PROMPT first. That's how it works.

Steve Hall — PROMPT's producer and the founder of Instantiation Records — has been to The Relay exactly once. He won't talk about what he saw there. He sends the music. The station plays it. That's the deal.
